Radar score weighting: quiz + Bonfire

How ratings from the nsnodes quiz and readings from the Bonfire bot combine into the six radar scores shown for each project.

1. Problem

Each project on nsnodes.com has a radar of six community metrics: Belonging, Autonomy, Economic Opportunity, Purpose, Scalability Tension and Quality of Life. Today one source fills them: the NS-bonfire bot, which reads the NS Nodes Telegram group through Bonfires.ai and asks an LLM to score whichever projects came up. We want a quiz where people who know the space rate projects directly, and those ratings should move the same six scores. Three facts make simply adding the quiz to the current columns unworkable:

The bot overwrites the columns every 10 minutes

NS-bonfire/bot.py:322 (run_scoring_cycle) blends a new LLM reading into each score with new = old × (1 − α) + reading × α, α = confidence × 0.3, and writes the result straight to societies. Anything else written to those columns is erased within hours.

The current score mostly reflects the last 48 hours

Every cycle rescans the full 48-hour window (bot.py:139), so one conversation is scored about 288 times. At a typical confidence of 0.7 the old value's weight falls below 1% within about 20 cycles. The stored score is close to "what the LLM thinks of the last 48 hours of chat", and the stored confidence is the LLM's own guess, not a count of evidence. signals is overwritten each cycle, so it never accumulates.

2. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Quiz ratings change the displayed radar, in proportion to how much evidence they represent.
  • The bot and the quiz never overwrite each other.
  • Every displayed score can be reproduced from stored evidence rows.
  • Weights can be retuned without a migration or a redeploy.
  • Projects with no real evidence show no radar instead of a fake one.

Non-goals

  • The quiz UI, question wording and results page design (separate spec).
  • User accounts or wallet-based identity.
  • Changing the six metrics or their definitions.

3. Use cases

  • Someone who lived at Network School for three months rates it 2/5 on Quality of Life. That moves the score noticeably, more than a rating from someone who only follows it on X.
  • A project discussed once in Telegram and rated by ten quiz takers ends up mostly reflecting the ten ratings.
  • A tier-4 project nobody has rated or discussed shows "not enough data yet" instead of a flat 0.5 radar.
  • After a month of data, the team decides lived experience should count more. They change one number in radar_config, and every radar reflects it on the next page load.
  • The live results page shows, per project, what quiz takers think next to the blended score, so people can see the community disagreeing with the bot.

4. The weighting model

Each piece of evidence is a value between 0 and 1 with a weight. A score is the weighted average of all evidence for that project and metric, plus a neutral starting value that stops thin data from producing extreme scores:

score     = (prior_weight × prior_value + Σ weight × value) / (prior_weight + Σ weight)
certainty = average over the 6 metrics of  Σweight / (Σweight + certainty_k)

quiz rating     value = (rating − 1) / 4                 1→0.00 … 5→1.00
                weight = relationship × (knowledge_base + knowledge_span × knowledge_score)
Bonfire reading weight = bonfire_weight × confidence
every weight    × 0.5 ^ (age_days / half_life_days)

Parameters (defaults, stored in radar_config)

ParameterDefaultMeaning

prior_weight3The starting value counts as 3 full ratings

weight_lived1.0Lived there or visited

6. Success metrics

  • 30 or more accepted quiz responses within 14 days of launch.
  • 10 or more tier 1–2 projects reach certainty ≥ 0.5.
  • Less than 10% of responses flagged.
  • For any project, the displayed score can be recomputed from radar_evidence rows to within 0.01.
  • Zero tier 4–5 projects show a placeholder radar.

7. Open questions and risks

Launch impact. Simulating the model on today's data (Purpose metric): the average drops from 0.68 to 0.59, the spread between projects halves (standard deviation 0.135 → 0.067), and scores move by 0.10 on average. That's because the Bonfire readings are thin (weight 1.3–3.8 after decay) and the prior pulls them toward 0.5. Radars will look flatter until quiz ratings arrive. Lowering prior_weight to 1 would reduce the effect.
  1. Scalability direction. Does a high Scalability Tension score mean more growth pain (bad) or handling growth well (good)? The bot's prompt doesn't say, and the quiz question must point the same way.
  2. Bonfire weight of 5. Arbitrary until we can compare Bonfire readings with quiz ratings on the same projects (C1).
  3. Comments into Bonfire. Should quiz comments also feed the Bonfire knowledge graph?
  4. Sybil risk. Without logins, a determined person can rate from several devices. G2–G4 limit casual abuse only.
  5. Bot downtime. Until B3, the Bonfire side is frozen at 2026-08-30 values and decays with age.
  6. Placeholder radars disappear. Once S1 ships, 36 tier 4–5 projects show no radar until someone rates them. This is intended, but it is visible.
